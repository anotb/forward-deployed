# From quote drafting to a useful design conversation

Read this when several plausible concepts emerge from the same discovery. The case and evidence below are fictional teaching inputs, not a customer result. The purpose is to show how evidence selects an experience, including when the ambitious idea should lose.

## The supplied situation

An industrial supplier wants to draft quotations faster. Sales assembles prior quotes; application engineers check specifications and exceptions; planners investigate component availability. A case walkthrough and interviews supply these facts for the example:

- In the delayed case, writing the final quote took little time. Three clarification rounds concerned which pressure requirement was essential and whether the requested delivery date could move.
- The engineer found a technically attractive substitute in an old quote. Its revision was no longer approved. A precedent helped locate an option but could not authorize it.
- A planner could check current stock, but a firm delivery promise also required assembly capacity and an approved route. Inventory alone did not settle availability.
- Sales sometimes left small inquiries unanswered because investigation consumed the same scarce engineering attention as large inquiries. No evidence yet establishes how many of those inquiries would convert.
- Engineers work in an existing case view. Customers receive proposals through the established sales process. No customer-facing access arrangement has been agreed.

One delayed case exposes a mechanism; it does not establish its prevalence. The team can develop a concept now while investigating whether these are recurring conditions.

## Three directions from that evidence

**Faster approved-quote preparation.** Assemble the accepted configuration, commercial terms and source references into the existing quote. This reduces administrative effort and inconsistent transfer. It does little for the observed clarification delay. It would become the strongest choice if representative cases showed that configuration was usually settled and document preparation consumed the useful decision window. A process change or existing template feature might deliver most of that benefit.

**An engineer's option investigation.** Interpret an incomplete request, identify the constraint whose clarification would change the design, retrieve applicable approved configurations and prepare feasible alternatives. The service could compare keeping the requirement with a later date, using an approved substitute, or relaxing a negotiable requirement. Its mechanism is less repeated investigation and better choices while the customer is still buying. That matches the delayed case and gives the intelligence consequential work.

**A customer configuration service.** Let customers explore the same alternatives directly and bring sales a better-developed request. This might widen access to expertise, especially for smaller inquiries. It also introduces unproven demand, permitted-knowledge boundaries and expectations about what constitutes a commitment. The supplied evidence does not justify making this the first release. An internal option service can teach whether alternatives are useful before the supplier invests in customer participation.

The second direction is the recommended first investigation. The third is a possible extension, not additional benefit to add to the second. Drafting remains a component of the accepted offer rather than a separate investment with the same savings counted again.

## Make the proposed experience inspectable

A sales engineer opens the request in the existing case. The assistant highlights the ambiguous pressure condition and explains why its interpretation separates two configuration families. The engineer clarifies that the higher pressure is preferred rather than mandatory. The assistant prepares two approved options and excludes the old unapproved substitute, preserving it only as a rejected precedent with a reason.

Each option distinguishes technical feasibility from delivery status. Where the planner has supplied a current commitment, the option displays it with its applicable configuration and time. Otherwise it identifies the remaining planning question. The engineer can change the requirement and see which options remain feasible. Sales accepts a proposal through the existing quote process. The system has prepared a better commercial conversation; it has not independently made the promise.

The business objects matter: a customer requirement, product variant, approved revision, inventory position, planning commitment and commercial offer are related but not interchangeable. A change to an approved revision or commitment should identify which pending option needs reconsideration.

## A first scope that preserves the idea

Use one product family and engineer-reviewed alternatives. Work through a straightforward request, an ambiguous requirement, an attractive but unapproved substitute and a delivery position that changes during preparation. These contrasts expose whether the service can advance the investigation coherently. A count of generated alternatives would not establish usefulness; experts need to find the options feasible and meaningfully different, and sales needs to use them in the decision window.

If a probe finds that unmarked drawings are unreliable but structured specifications plus engineer-marked regions work, redesign intake around that assistance. If planning data cannot establish meaningful delivery options, offer technical configuration assistance and leave the delivery investigation with the planner. If the remaining manual investigation still dominates the delay, that narrower scope may no longer justify the investment. Do not retain the original commercial promise after removing the capability that made it plausible.

If broader sampling instead shows that clarification is rare and quote assembly dominates, prefer the first direction. If internal use demonstrates useful options and small customers repeatedly engage with a supported prototype, reconsider the third. Those are changes in evidence and service design, not a requirement to progress through three maturity stages.
