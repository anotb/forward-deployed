# Three enterprise opportunities

These are fictional design situations. Use the reasoning to develop an opportunity from the supplied evidence; the industries and first scopes are examples.

## Delegate procurement through to an executable plan

A company opening new locations asks for faster purchasing summaries. The useful work happens before and after the summary: interpreting an operating plan, deciding which requirements can share a purchase, finding compatible products, comparing supplier terms, and dealing with changes before opening day. Experienced buyers perform this work for the largest projects; smaller teams improvise.

One option is an assistant that retrieves purchasing guidance. A stronger opportunity is a procurement assignment that continues across the plan, catalog, budget, and purchasing system. The employee delegates “prepare this location to open,” supplies the operating plan, and receives a feasible purchasing plan with the dependencies resolved.

The agent turns the plan into requirements, reconciles them with existing assets and approved catalogs, investigates alternatives, and creates the corresponding purchase requests. When an item becomes unavailable, it revisits affected choices and shows the cost and schedule consequences. Employees can change an opening date or standard and explore the revised plan. The result is an editable working plan connected to transactions.

The intelligence lies in interpreting intent, reconciling unlike descriptions, finding alternatives, and following the consequences of a change. Deterministic checks handle arithmetic, budgets, and permitted transactions. The buyer sets the commercial authority within which the assignment can proceed.

A useful first build handles one location and two purchasing categories through to valid requests in the test system. Include a substitution and a changed requirement. Judge the package by whether purchasing can execute it and how much reconstruction remains. If the biggest obstacle proves to be an unavailable catalog interface, resolve that interface or change the funded scope before claiming faster openings.

The larger possibility is purchasing expertise for every opening and expansion, including projects too small to receive a dedicated buyer. Size the opportunity through that additional coverage and completed work.

## Investigate retail performance as conditions change

Category managers receive weekly performance packs. They investigate a few large movements; most local changes never receive an explanation. The request is to summarize the pack.

Develop an investigation service that works across sales, availability, promotions, price changes, and customer feedback. For a falling category, it forms competing explanations, runs the queries needed to separate them, and follows the strongest leads. A sales decline coinciding with a promotion may reflect stockouts, substitution, changed demand, or a reporting defect. Each explanation implies different work.

The service maintains the investigation as new data arrives. It retires disproven explanations, notices when a previously selected action no longer fits, and builds a comparison the category manager can manipulate. The manager can change a promotion assumption, inspect a group of stores, or turn a finding into a replenishment or merchandising task.

The opportunity is broader analytical coverage and a shorter interval between a change and a useful response. Make the cost of follow-through visible: more findings can require buying, store, or supplier work. Prioritization should favor decisions that have a remaining action window.

For the first deployment, choose one category with accessible transaction and stock data. Replay past episodes with later information withheld, then run alongside the category team. Compare explanations and resulting actions with its existing process. Track useful investigations that would otherwise have been skipped, the time to act, and the work created for receiving teams.

## Turn specialist training into a personal rehearsal service

A training provider runs workshops for managers handling difficult commercial conversations. Its expertise lives in facilitators who notice a missed signal, alter the scenario, and explain the consequence. Individual practice is expensive, so most learners receive the same scenario and little repetition.

AI can make that expertise available as repeated practice around the learner's upcoming work. A rehearsal uses the customer's product, procedures, and situation; the participant's choices change the counterpart's behavior and the next problem. The learner can stop, inspect a decision, try another approach, or request coaching. A facilitator can edit the scenario and the criteria used to assess the performance.

This creates a new recurring service for the provider and expands employees' access to coaching. Its substance is the adaptive exercise and feedback, including consequences that develop over several turns. Merely changing names in a standard script would add little.

Build one rehearsal from a situation the facilitators know well. Have them test whether it responds coherently to different approaches and gives useful feedback, then let learners use it before a relevant assignment. Compare performance on a fresh scenario and observe whether they return for preparation. Price ongoing customer-context updates and facilitator support alongside model use. That first experience tests educational value and recurring demand before building a large scenario catalog.

## Reading that can widen the design

[OpenAI's enterprise research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-b2b-signals/) discusses deeper, delegated work across business functions. [Its research on changing work](https://openai.com/index/unlocking-new-ways-of-working/) examines recurring tasks outside workers' usual occupations. These support looking at the assignment and the employee's expanding role when finding opportunities.

[Anthropic's agent patterns](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) help distinguish a known sequence from work requiring adaptive investigation. [Its context-engineering guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) explains how a continuing assignment can find and retain the information it needs. The concepts above are original design examples, not reported customer results from those sources.
