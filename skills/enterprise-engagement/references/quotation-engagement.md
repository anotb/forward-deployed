# An opening proposition that changes after discovery

This fictional industrial-supplier example uses invented source excerpts. It illustrates engagement judgment, not a required proposal structure or a claim about deployment results.

The field team has been asked to prepare a customer conversation about an AI quotation service. The first idea is to generate quotes faster. The available material complicates it:

> Sales brief: “Custom requests take three days. Competitors respond the same day. We want an instant quote demonstration.”
>
> Quote specialist, interview: “Writing the quote takes me about twelve minutes once engineering has settled the configuration. We don't log a quotation until we think we can supply it.”
>
> Lost-request email: “No response from engineering on the substituted seal. Customer has ordered elsewhere.”
>
> Engineering manager: “The standard configurations are straightforward. The awkward requests need someone to work out which substitution is permissible. I won't approve more requests just because an AI sends them faster.”
>
> Product administrator: “The current sales application supports a draft-quote extension. A planner must confirm capacity before a delivery date is offered. I can show you the extension; I have not reserved implementation time.”

## The thesis worth taking into the room

The twelve-minute estimate does not establish a baseline, and one lost request does not establish the size of unserved demand. They do weaken the assumption that document preparation is the principal constraint. The quote register also excludes some requests the proposed service is supposed to recover. A proof using only accepted standard quotations would select away the difficult work.

A more useful thesis is: help the supplier respond to requests currently delayed or declined by investigating configuration alternatives and preparing the specific decision engineering and planning need to make. The opening episode is an awkward substitution request, not a clean specification converted into attractive prose.

Ask the engineering manager to compare a rejected request with a similar one they accepted. If the difference is a retrievable compatibility fact, the service may assemble a supported alternative. If the difference is an unavailable physical test or a commercial exception only the manager can authorize, the service may prepare that decision but cannot remove it. Those answers change the offer.

## An offer connected to the uncertainty

An instant-quote demonstration would prove that a document can be generated. The investment question is whether useful alternatives can be prepared without consuming the same scarce engineering effort or making unsupported capacity promises.

Recommend a bounded investigation and prototype around one configuration family with accessible compatibility information. Include requests that were never quoted, with customer-sensitive information handled under the engagement's existing access arrangements. Reconstruct what an expert needed, try preparing the alternatives, and let the receiving specialist assess whether the result advances the decision. Include a case where the correct outcome is a focused information request or a justified decline. More generated quotes are not necessarily more useful work.

Use the existing draft-quote extension if inspection shows it can carry alternatives, unresolved questions and the planner's confirmation. That is a deployment hypothesis, not a finding from the administrator's comment. A separate workbench becomes reasonable if the extension cannot support the investigation or the relevant users; a maintained product already covering this behavior could remove the need for either custom approach.

The customer contribution matters to the offer. The field team can prepare the experience and technical probe. The customer must make representative requests and applicable compatibility information available, arrange specialist review, and involve the person who owns capacity confirmation. The administrator's willingness to demonstrate an extension is not an engineering allocation. Obtain that assessment before putting an integration date in the offer.

## Example customer-facing recommendation

> We propose testing whether the team can answer difficult configuration requests it currently leaves unresolved. The first version would prepare supported alternatives and the remaining decisions inside your quotation workflow; engineering would retain approval of exceptions and planning would confirm delivery commitments.
>
> The next commitment funds the investigation and prototype for the selected configuration family. It requires access to accepted and lost requests, the information used to judge substitutions, and review time from engineering and planning. We will use that work to recommend whether to extend the current sales application, use an existing product capability, or stop this approach. A wider deployment and its delivery date depend on that finding and the application team's assessment.
>
> The commercial benefit to test is a useful response to previously stalled demand without a matching increase in specialist effort. We cannot yet promise a conversion uplift or same-day quotations.

This recommendation is not always the right one. If the comparison shows that ordinary quote preparation dominates a high-volume operation and accepted inputs are already complete, a native drafting workflow could justify investment sooner. If every lost request requires scarce physical testing, improving intake and allocation may be more valuable than an AI quotation service. The engagement should follow the mechanism revealed by the evidence.
