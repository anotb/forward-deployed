# From a company assistant to delegated release planning

This fictional engagement follows an enterprise software company choosing how to deepen AI adoption. The records and dialogue are invented.

## The opening request

The company has enterprise AI access for its 900 employees. Its technology sponsor asks for a company-wide knowledge assistant and an opening deck showing the benefits. Existing material includes:

> Sponsor: "People still ask colleagues where things are. We should connect the company knowledge and let them ask AI."
>
> Product operations lead: "My best use is preparing a release. I give AI the launch brief, open work and recent decisions, then ask it to find contradictions. It takes several rounds, but it catches things I miss."
>
> Customer success lead: "The release note can be correct and still be wrong for a customer. We need to know which deployment, contract and configuration it applies to."
>
> Engineering lead: "I get a list of red items before every launch. Some are obsolete; some concern a different customer cohort. Someone has to investigate which ones matter."

The opening request describes a useful access problem. The practitioner demonstrates a larger opportunity: AI can help maintain an account of what a release makes possible for specific customers and the work still needed to deliver it. That assignment crosses product, engineering and customer success. Better search is an ingredient, but the finished output is a launch plan people can act on.

## Develop the stronger proposition

Ask the product operations lead to demonstrate the whole assignment. Inspect what they give the model, why they reject parts of its first answer, how they find missing evidence and what they finally share. The repeatable practice may include a context structure and reasoning task that other teams could use immediately with their existing AI access.

Take a recognizable release into the opening conversation. Show the AI interpreting the intended customer population, finding the relevant engineering and security evidence, investigating inconsistent status and producing a cohort-specific readiness brief. Introduce a changed requirement and show which conclusions and tasks should change. This makes delegated work tangible.

The initial customer offer becomes:

> Start with release planning in one product group. Give the team a shared AI workspace that develops and maintains the readiness brief, investigates conflicts across the source material and prepares the work needed to close them. Product and engineering leaders keep their release decisions; the agent takes on the repeated investigation and coordination around those decisions.
>
> We will adapt the successful employee practice, connect the sources needed for this assignment and work through upcoming launches with the team. The initial deployment should establish which work can be delegated, how much expert review it consumes and which elements can be reused across the other product groups.

The company-wide adoption effort still has a role. Teach other employees the demonstrated pattern of assigning an outcome, supplying useful context and inspecting the result. Give the release team deeper support because its recurring work justifies shared state and integration. Access, learning and deployment reinforce the same change in how people work.

## Make the buying decision concrete

The customer supplies launch material, source access and the people who understand release applicability. The delivery team develops the experience, tools and evaluation cases. The platform owner helps choose a supported operating route. Existing enterprise workspace capabilities may be sufficient for shared investigation; automatic task updates and event-driven refresh depend on the available integration surface.

A useful proof has the team use the resulting plan. Can they identify which customers are ready, see why, and act on the remaining work without reconstructing the whole investigation? Does a later source change cause the relevant conclusion to be revisited? Those questions connect the technical work to the investment.

The strongest alternative is an improved team practice using existing tools. If a maintained brief and reusable assignment already remove most reconstruction, spread that approach. Build persistent agent behavior when repeated refresh, investigation and cross-system actions create enough additional value. The engagement should retain the ambitious outcome while choosing the delivery method the work earns.
