# Redesign a product-research workflow

Use this constructed example when a deployed AI workflow produces faster drafts but changes people's work in unexpected ways.

## Follow the work through the team

A product organization introduces an AI research workspace. It can search interview material, compare segments, examine usage data, and prepare findings and prototype ideas. Previously, researchers synthesized interviews, analysts supplied a metric readout, designers explored options, and product managers assembled the decision brief.

The opportunity is to let a small team investigate a question and test several responses in one shared workspace. Researchers can spend more time on unresolved questions; analysts can develop discriminating tests; designers can explore alternatives earlier. Begin with that changed way of working.

## Diagnose the pilot's uneven results

Routine questions move faster, but senior researchers avoid the more complex AI findings. Observation reveals that conflicting interview evidence has been compressed into a single theme. Reviewers reopen recordings and recreate the segment analysis.

For twelve illustrative investigations, labor in minutes per investigation is:

| Work | Previous preparation + review | AI preparation + review | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eight routine questions | 60 + 20 | 25 + 20 | Source links and segments are adequate for the decision. |
| Four questions with conflicting evidence | 75 + 35 | 35 + 90 | Reviewers reconstruct which users said what and whether apparent disagreement reflects different needs. |

Preparation falls by 440 minutes. Review grows by 220 minutes. Total labor falls from 1,080 to 860 minutes, but the complex cases become 15 minutes slower each. An aggregate saving conceals the precise work that the senior team still cannot delegate. These sample values illustrate the calculation, not a population estimate.

## Change the capability and the responsibility

Preserve conflicting findings with their participant segment and source. Let the agent investigate whether the disagreement reflects different roles, product versions, or evidence quality. Give the researcher a view of those distinctions and the next question that could settle them. Make correction update the shared investigation so later analysis and prototypes use it.

The product manager should be able to move from the maintained investigation into alternative solutions without rebuilding a slide deck. Remove the duplicate synthesis step once the shared artifact serves its purpose. Agree how the team will use the released capacity, such as testing more ideas or investigating neglected segments.

If the agent can only summarize interviews today, improve the context and analysis tools needed for the intended investigation. Extra reviewer effort should reveal the next capability to build.

## Teach the new decisions

Give participants a case where administrators want more setup control while invited users want fewer setup steps. Ask them to complete the investigation and propose a prototype experiment.

A strong response preserves the role difference, examines whether usage evidence supports it, and develops an experience that can be tested with each group. It does not average the requests into a generic “simplify onboarding” recommendation. Follow with a case in which both groups share the same obstacle, so the method remains open to a simpler explanation.

Observe the team's whole interaction with the artifact: can the researcher correct a finding, the analyst extend a query, and the designer revise the prototype without reconstructing prior work?

## Use the new capacity

Track accepted investigations, total effort, decisions informed, and experiments launched. Include the questions the team can now examine that were previously deferred. If the new workspace works but the organization still requires every old handoff, change that operating arrangement. If teams cannot independently extend an investigation, improve the method and context with them.
