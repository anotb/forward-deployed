# Expand a practice by testing what should transfer

Use this example when a local AI practice has become useful and another team wants it. Expansion should grow capability and teach the organization where reuse pays.

## Choose a next team for a reason

A product-discovery team uses a shared AI workspace to investigate customer needs, inspect usage data, and build prototypes. Another product group wants the same result. It serves developers through an API, so much of its evidence is in code examples, developer discussions, and integration failures rather than interviews and screen flows.

This is a useful next deployment because the investigation method may transfer while its evidence and artifacts change. Copying the original workspace would leave the new team with the wrong context and a prototype format unsuited to its work.

A different expansion could deepen the original team's work, such as maintaining investigations as new evidence arrives. Choose between breadth and depth from the next outcome's value and the constraint it exposes.

## Adapt the working method

Keep the method of forming hypotheses, testing them against evidence, comparing alternatives, and making an artifact that supports a decision. Replace local product definitions, source maps, examples, and access with the developer-product team's context.

For one API onboarding question, the new team asks the agent to reproduce an integration failure, compare the intended and observed behavior, and prepare a runnable example of an improved experience. The artifact may be a small client application and documentation change. The definition of a useful prototype has changed; the inquiry method has not.

Have a member of the receiving team run the task and improve the asset. The original team can help explain the method, but the test of transfer is whether the receiving team can perform and adapt the work through its normal tools.

## Identify the new cost

The expanded practice may need a package registry, a reproducible test environment, or better source retrieval. Compare the cost of those additions with the expected investigation value. A shared instruction may save task-design effort while environment setup dominates the next deployment.

At larger scale, inspect what becomes scarce: domain review, access support, compute, source maintenance, or time to act on findings. Make the next investment where it increases completed work. Higher usage is useful evidence about demand; it does not identify the bottleneck by itself.

## Let the receiving team invent

Preserve room for discoveries that improve the shared method. The developer-product team may create a way to turn an observed error into a reproducible test that the original product team can also use. Feed that method back into the shared assets with an example and an owner.

Use demonstrations of completed work to attract the next team, and make the assets discoverable through existing communities. Track successful reuse, adaptations, and continuing use on consequential tasks. Keep changes local where business meaning differs; promote improvements that survive the next variation.

For expansion into another customer account, establish its outcome, operating environment, data permissions, and support arrangement on their own terms. Reuse the method and assets the organization has rights to share. Evaluate onboarding and support effort as part of the offer.
